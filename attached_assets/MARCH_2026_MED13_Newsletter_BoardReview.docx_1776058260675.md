**A MESSAGE FROM OUR FOUNDER**

## Dear *MED13 Families*,

When our son was diagnosed with MED13 syndrome in 2024, there was no foundation, no research cohort, and almost no infrastructure to find other families like ours. There were a handful of medical case reports and a lot of unanswered questions. We knew almost nothing about the range of how this condition affects children or what might one day help them.

In October 2025, we officially launched the MED13 Foundation. In just a few months, more has happened than I dared hope for in our first year. This letter is a chance to share it all with you: what we’ve done, what we’ve learned, and what’s coming next.

None of it is possible without this community.

**OUR STORY**

## How the *Foundation* Began

MED13 syndrome was first described in the medical literature in 2018, which means the entire history of this diagnosis fits within a few years. With roughly 200 diagnosed individuals worldwide, it is an ultra rare disease: too small for most research programs to notice, but affecting real children and real families in very real ways.

My background is in epidemiology and disease research. I spent years at Stanford and the Chan Zuckerberg Initiative building research infrastructure for other diseases. When our son was diagnosed, I realized those skills — patient cohort building, data infrastructure, scientific collaboration — were exactly what our community needed.

|  | *“We decided not to wait for the research to find us. We decided to build the infrastructure that would make research possible.”* — Amy Krystosik, President & Co-founder |
| :---- | :---- |

The MED13 Foundation was co-founded by families of children with MED13. We are parent-led and family-driven, with a clear focus: build the research tools that will enable treatments, and connect every family who needs us.

**WHAT WE’VE ACCOMPLISHED**

## Milestones Since We *Launched*

* **Launched a research data partnership and biorepository with COMBINEDBrain —** We have partnered with COMBINEDBrain to establish 1\) a structured natural history data study in Matrix and 2\) a biorepository for MED13 families. This will become the foundational dataset that researchers need to study the condition and we are actively growing participation.

* **Rare Village is our Fiscal Sponsor —** We have partnered with Rare Village to establish regulatory oversight of our finances and enable tax-deductible donations. **We** **raised $35,000 in our first months —** Including one sustaining donor at $200/month. Every dollar is directly funding our research infrastructure.

* **Formed our Scientific Advisory Board —** Five world-class scientists spanning brain organoids, mouse models, molecular mechanisms, and clinical genetics. See full roster below.

* **Launched our Monthly Family Meetup —** Fifteen families now gather virtually each month to share experiences, ask questions, and support one another.

* **Launched a scientific advisory partnership with CureNDD —** Working with Dr. Jin Lee, PhD to connect MED13 to broader neurodevelopmental disorder research networks.

## MED13 at the *Festival of Biologics,* San Diego, CA

Amy represented the foundation on two panels at Festival of Biologics, San Diego (March 2026\) — MED13 Foundation was in the room with 1,500+ biopharma leaders.

* **Panel 1**: “How Cross-Sector Partnerships Are Advancing Genetic Medicine in Rare Disease” with Jin Lee (Cure NDD), Jeff Milton (La Jolla Labs), Anahid Foroughishafiei (former n-Lorem), and Kieran Vu (BioLogic). Topics: natural history data, biobanking, biomarker studies, regulatory literacy, governance, and de-risking investment in ultra-rare diseases.

* **Panel 2 — keynote AI panel:** AI across the therapeutic value chain with Jonathan D. Ziegler (Cradle); Sophia Humphreys (MJH Life Sciences) and Philip M. Arlen (Precision Biologics). Amy shared work on how AI can help us build knowledge graphs with sparse rare disease data and her work on VariantFormer, a foundation model trained on the largest paired WGS/RNA-seq dataset for rare variants. Two panels, same through-line: the infrastructure matters as much as the science.

## MED13 at the *Rare Bootcamp,* Boston, MA

Amy represented the foundation at the bootcamp sponsored by Ultragenyx and Every Life Foundation. We joined a cohort of families, researchers and partners building the infrastructure to make rare disease drug development possible.

* **Collaboration across rare conditions accelerates progress.** Active dialogue with the MED13L Foundation, MED12 Foundaiton and other CDK8-module disease communities.
